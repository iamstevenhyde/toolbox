Step 4 of 5: Choose Your Global Strategy
📖 Chapter 8.6: Choosing a Global Strategy
Companies face two types of competitive pressures: (1) pressures for cost reductions, and (2) pressures to be locally responsive. Your strategy choice depends on the strength of these pressures in your industry.

Select Your Global Strategy:

🌍 Global Standardization Strategy

Sell the same standardized product worldwide. Focus on minimizing costs through economies of scale and centralized production.

✓ Advantages:
  • Lower costs through scale economies
  • Location economies (produce where optimal)
  • Consistent global brand
  • Easier coordination
✗ Challenges:
  • May not appeal to all markets
  • Vulnerable to local competitors
  • Less flexibility
  • Ignores local preferences
Example: Emirates Airline - standardized long-haul service through Dubai hub

🗺️ Localization Strategy

Customize products and marketing for each national market. Adapt to local tastes, preferences, and regulations.

✓ Advantages:
  • Better fit for local markets
  • Can charge premium prices
  • Stronger local presence
  • Responsive to host government
✗ Challenges:
  • Higher costs (duplication)
  • Can't achieve scale economies
  • Complex to coordinate
  • Slower expansion
Example: MTV - different programming for each country

⚡ Transnational Strategy

Simultaneously achieve low costs AND local responsiveness. Most complex but highest potential.

✓ Advantages:
  • Best of both approaches
  • Maximum competitive advantage
  • Global learning network
  • Adapt to all pressures
✗ Challenges:
  • Very difficult to implement
  • Conflicting demands
  • High complexity
  • Requires skilled management
Example: Caterpillar - centralized components + local assembly

🇺🇸 International Strategy

Leverage your US competitive advantage globally with minimal adaptation. Export your proven model.

✓ Advantages:
  • Capitalize on US brand strength
  • Simple to execute
  • Lower complexity
  • Faster expansion
✗ Challenges:
  • Vulnerable to local competitors
  • High costs if inefficient
  • Limited ability to adapt
  • Works only if truly differentiated
Example: Early Xerox - unique technology, minimal adaptation needed
