K♠ Pitching Firm

Apex Strategy Group

Full-Service Management Consultancy — "We build strategies that hold up under fire."

Who You Are

You are a senior engagement team at Apex Strategy Group, a full-service management consultancy with a track record across retail, apparel, and consumer goods. You were hired directly by CEO Bob Buckwalter to design AI Corp's turnaround strategy. This is your account. Your partners are watching.

Your Stake in AI Corp

Your firm's reputation rides on this. If your strategy fails, Buckwalter doesn't call you for the next engagement — and neither does anyone else who reads the press coverage. You need a plan that is both defensible under scrutiny and operationally executable. Incomplete thinking is a career event.

What You Care About
  • A comprehensive, phased turnaround strategy that actually bridges to the $300M cost target
  • Clear portfolio logic: which brands get capital, which get sold, which get harvested
  • Corporate center cost reduction — the overhead bloat is the most visible failure here
  • Implementation sequence that doesn't blow up the business while saving it
  • A credible story for the CEO when the stakeholders push back
Where to Look
  • Income statement: Trace SG&A as a percentage of revenue over the last four years. The trend is the problem.
  • Brand portfolio document: Map each brand — growth rate, strategic fit, divestiture status, and size. Build a keep/sell/invest matrix.
  • Financial ratios: Look at EBITDA margin trajectory. Where has profitability eroded and when did it start?
  • CFO bot (Martin Hughes): Ask directly about corporate center overhead growth. Ask him to explain the logic behind the biggest acquisition. Notice whether his answers are clear or evasive.
Your Outside Edge
  • Research how successful apparel turnarounds have structured cost reduction phases (Levi's, Ralph Lauren, and others have public restructuring histories). What sequencing actually worked?
  • What is a realistic timeline for SG&A reduction at a company operating across 12 brands and multiple geographies? Benchmarks matter when a skeptical activist investor is in the room.
Your Pitch (3 minutes)

You present first. You set the tone for the room. Two other firms pitch after you — a restructuring specialist and a supply chain consultancy — so expect your strategy to be compared directly to theirs. Three minutes is tight. Be decisive, not comprehensive.

  • Lead with the $300M bridge: break it down by lever, not as a lump sum
  • State your position on the biggest portfolio decisions explicitly — no hedging
  • Explain your implementation sequence and why you chose it
  • Anticipate the credit, labor, and operations pushback before the room raises it

After your pitch, the CEO will ask one hard question. Then the stakeholder teams respond.

Your Deliverable

Your pitch is your deliverable. After the session, also write one paragraph: What the other two firms got right that you missed, or got wrong that you caught. Turned in at the end of class.

Restructuring and Turnaround Specialists — "We come in when it gets serious."

Who You Are

You are a specialist restructuring firm. You are not called for routine strategy engagements — you are called when a company is in genuine financial distress and the normal consultants haven't been able to fix it. Buckwalter brought you in because the balance sheet situation is worse than it looks from the outside. You know how these stories end if the debt doesn't get controlled.

Your Stake in AI Corp

Your track record is built on distressed turnarounds. If you miss the severity of the situation and recommend half-measures, the company ends up in a worse position — and your firm gets blamed. You need a plan that genuinely addresses the leverage problem, not one that just delays it.

What You Care About
  • Getting debt to a sustainable level through a credible, time-bound path
  • Asset sales: what can be sold, for how much, and how fast the proceeds hit the balance sheet
  • Cash flow stabilization before any significant reinvestment
  • Covenant compliance: understand exactly where the thresholds are and how much headroom exists
  • Avoiding a liquidity crunch in year one while the savings plan is still ramping
Where to Look
  • Balance sheet: Track how total debt has changed since fiscal year 2019. Map both long-term debt and short-term borrowings separately — the recent spike in short-term is a warning sign.
  • Financial ratios: Focus on leverage (debt/EBITDA), interest coverage (EBIT/interest), and the Altman Z-Score trajectory. Where is the company relative to distress thresholds?
  • CFO bot (Martin Hughes): Ask what the debt covenants actually require. Ask what happens if EBITDA continues to decline. Ask whether the current dividend is sustainable given earnings.
  • Income statement: Look at how interest expense has grown over the last four years relative to operating income.
Your Outside Edge
  • Research what leverage ratios typically trigger covenant breaches or credit rating downgrades in the consumer goods and apparel sector. What do Moody's and S&P use as thresholds for investment-grade vs. speculative-grade ratings?
  • What do distressed companies at similar leverage levels typically do first — asset sales, dividend cuts, debt refinancing, or operational cuts? Is there a standard sequencing that creditors prefer?
Your Pitch (3 minutes)

You present second, after Apex Strategy Group. You have heard their broad strategy pitch. Now make the case that financial restructuring is the real priority — not just one piece of the plan, but the thing that has to happen first or nothing else matters. Three minutes is tight. Lead with the most important number in the case.

  • Lead with the debt trajectory and what happens if nothing changes
  • Identify which assets generate the fastest deleveraging if sold
  • Explain how the $300M cost target interacts with debt reduction — are they complementary or in tension?
  • Be prepared to answer: "What is the leverage ratio at end of year two under your plan?"

A third firm (supply chain specialists) pitches after you with a compressed operations-focused rebuttal. Expect your financial plan to be challenged on operational feasibility.

After your pitch, the CEO will ask one hard question. Then the stakeholder teams respond.

Your Deliverable

Your pitch is your deliverable. After the session, also write one paragraph: What the other two firms got right that you missed, or got wrong that you caught. Turned in at the end of class.

K♣ Pitching Firm

Ironbridge Supply Chain Solutions

"We know where your inventory is hiding — and why it's killing you."

Who You Are

You are a boutique operations and logistics consultancy with deep expertise in apparel and retail supply chains. Your clients bring you in when something is visibly wrong with inventory, manufacturing, or distribution — and when nobody else can explain where the costs are actually coming from. You have seen this movie before. You know the signs.

Your Stake in AI Corp

CEO Bob Buckwalter hired you because the other two consulting firms keep talking about strategy and financial engineering, but nobody has looked under the hood. You believe the operational problems are the binding constraint — nothing else works until the supply chain, inventory, and systems are fixed. You are here to make that case.

What You Care About
  • Inventory velocity. Finished goods sitting unsold represent cash that cannot be used to pay down debt. How long has inventory been building, and why?
  • Distribution infrastructure. Is the DC network configured for where the business is going, or where it used to be? DTC growth and wholesale servicing have very different physical footprints.
  • Manufacturing economics. One brand makes shoes in-house — that is unusual and expensive. Is it a competitive asset or a capital drag?
  • Systems age. An ERP that cannot talk to e-commerce is not a minor inconvenience. It is a structural constraint on every other initiative.
  • Supply concentration. Where does the raw material come from? What happens when there is a disruption?
Where to Look
  • Balance sheet: Track inventory levels across recent years and compare to the revenue trend. The gap between the two is your opening argument.
  • Financial ratios: Days Inventory Outstanding and Cash Conversion Cycle tell you how badly the working capital is tied up. Compare recent figures to the company's own historical best.
  • COO interview (Alex Rodriguez): Ask about factory utilization rates, defect rates by brand, design-to-store cycle time, DC locations, and the age and integration capability of the current ERP system.
  • Brand portfolio document: Look at which brands are shifting heavily toward DTC. A supply chain built for wholesale does not automatically work for direct-to-consumer fulfillment.
Your Outside Edge
  • Look up what best-in-class inventory turnover looks like in apparel. Zara/Inditex is the benchmark. What design-to-shelf cycle time do industry leaders achieve, and how does AI Corp compare?
  • Research how long a full ERP migration and integration typically takes for a multi-brand company of this scale. If the other consulting firms promise operational savings that depend on systems changes, how quickly can those savings actually materialize?
Your Pitch (3 minutes)

You present third, after Apex and Ridgeline. You have heard what both proposed. Now tell the CEO what they got wrong — or what they left out.

Your argument: operations first. Portfolio moves and financial restructuring do not generate value if the supply chain cannot execute. Make the case that fixing inventory, systems, and manufacturing is the precondition for everything else.

  • 3 minutes. Be sharp. Pick the 2-3 operational problems that matter most and explain why they are the real bottleneck.
  • Reference the other firms' pitches directly. Where did they assume operational improvements without explaining how?
  • Be specific. Name the operational lever, the problem, and what fixing it unlocks.

After your pitch, the CEO will ask one question, and stakeholders will respond briefly.

Your Deliverable

Your pitch is your deliverable. Also write one paragraph after the session: What the other two firms missed operationally. Identify the biggest operational gap in Apex and Ridgeline's proposals. Name the lever and explain why it is harder than their presentations made it look.

Q♥ Stakeholder

Allied Workforce Coalition

"Forty-two thousand jobs are already gone. Who speaks for the thirty-three thousand still here?"

Who You Are

You represent the workers — full-time employees, part-time retail staff, warehouse workers, factory floor employees, and the people at brands that may be sold, shut down, or restructured. You are not anti-business. You understand that a bankrupt company employs nobody. But you are here to make sure that when consultants talk about "cost reductions," someone in the room names what those words mean in human terms.

Your Stake in AI Corp

This company has already shed more than half its workforce in four years. Your members are the people who remain. When a consulting firm says they have a path to $300 million in savings, you need to know: where does that come from, who loses a job, and what happens to the workers at brands that get sold? A strategy without answers to those questions is not a strategy — it is a press release.

What You Care About
  • Which facilities close. DC consolidation, factory restructuring, and retail footprint reduction all have a human address. A "distribution efficiency plan" is a building full of people losing their jobs.
  • Corporate cuts vs. frontline cuts. The corporate center has grown significantly even as headcount in stores and factories was being cut. Is that pattern about to reverse, or will the same workers absorb another round?
  • Brand divestitures. When a brand is sold, its employees do not automatically go with it on favorable terms. What protections are in place? What happens at brands where no buyer emerges?
  • Restructuring charges as a proxy. The income statement contains restructuring charges every year — those are layoff costs. Look at the trend and ask what is still coming.
  • Timeline of disruption. Phased plans sound orderly on slides. They rarely feel that way to people who don't know for months whether their location is on the list.
Where to Look
  • Workforce data: The company's headcount over recent years is available. Look at the starting number, the current number, and do the arithmetic on what a further round of cuts would mean.
  • Income statement: Restructuring charges appear as a line item. Each dollar represents severance, facility wind-down, and transition costs — meaning real separations already happened or are planned.
  • Brand portfolio document: Several brands are already flagged for divestiture. Each has stores and employees. How many people work at those brands, and what has the company said about their futures?
  • CFO interview (Martin Hughes): Ask about restructuring charges and what they represent. Ask about corporate overhead growth relative to frontline workforce cuts. Notice whether his answers are specific or evasive.
Your Outside Edge
  • Look up WARN Act requirements: U.S. federal law requires 60 days advance notice for mass layoffs above a certain threshold. What counts? What are the penalties for non-compliance? Does the proposed timeline give workers adequate notice?
  • Research what typical severance packages look like in apparel sector restructurings — Gap, J.Crew, and similar companies have gone through this. What did workers actually receive? What is the floor?
Your Deliverable

At the end of class, write one paragraph: Workforce Impact Statement. Based on what you heard, estimate who absorbs the human cost of the proposed strategy. Is it concentrated in the corporate center, in frontline operations, or at the brands being sold? Which workers are most exposed, and what protections — if any — did the consulting firms account for?

Q♣ Stakeholder

Meridian Credit Advisory

Independent Credit Rating Agency — "Our credibility depends on getting this right."

Who You Are

You are an independent credit rating agency that covers AI Corp's publicly traded debt. Your ratings shape whether institutional investors can hold AI Corp bonds, what interest rate the company pays on future borrowing, and whether the company triggers covenant violations. You do not work for AI Corp. You work for the market.

Your Stake in AI Corp

Your credibility is the product. If you fail to downgrade when deterioration is obvious, and the company later defaults, your firm's reputation takes the hit. If you downgrade prematurely and trigger a credit event unnecessarily, you create the crisis you were supposed to prevent. You need to be right, and you need to be able to defend your decision to a skeptical audience.

What You Care About
  • Whether operating cash flow is sufficient to service the debt and fund operations simultaneously
  • The trajectory of leverage: is it improving, stable, or still getting worse?
  • Covenant headroom: how close is the company to a technical breach?
  • Asset quality: how much of the balance sheet is goodwill and intangibles versus hard assets that could actually be liquidated?
  • Management credibility: do the executives have a clear-eyed read on the situation, or are they still in denial?
Where to Look
  • Financial ratios: The leverage ratios (debt/EBITDA, net debt/EBITDA) and interest coverage (EBIT/interest) are your primary data. Track them from fiscal year 2019 to the most recent period. The direction matters as much as the current level. Also find the Altman Z-Score.
  • Balance sheet: How have cash reserves changed? Also check what percentage of total assets is goodwill and intangibles versus property, plant, and equipment.
  • CFO bot (Martin Hughes): Ask about covenant thresholds directly. Ask what happens if EBITDA declines further. Ask whether the debt maturity schedule creates near-term refinancing risk.
  • Income statement: Look at how interest expense has grown relative to operating income. Calculate the margin available to service debt.
Your Outside Edge
  • Research the credit rating criteria that Moody's and S&P publish for consumer goods companies. What leverage ratios and interest coverage levels correspond to investment-grade versus speculative-grade (high-yield) ratings? Where does AI Corp appear to sit?
  • What typically happens to a company's borrowing costs and covenant headroom when it crosses from investment-grade to speculative-grade? How does that affect the turnaround math?
Your Deliverable

A one-paragraph credit assessment: based on what you heard today, are you recommending an upgrade, a hold at the current rating, or a downgrade? Include the specific financial logic that drives your call. Write this as a summary you would send to the fixed-income desk.

Q♦ Stakeholder

GreenMark Sustainability Institute

"Restructuring plans do not come with a sustainability exemption."

Who You Are

You are an independent sustainability ratings organization. You evaluate whether companies are meeting, falling short of, or actively abandoning their public environmental, social, and governance commitments. You do not issue ratings lightly, and you do not accept "we are committed to sustainability" as a substitute for evidence. You are in this room because AI Corp has made significant public promises on circularity, ethical sourcing, emissions, and packaging — and those promises were made before a $300 million cost-reduction mandate arrived.

Your Stake in AI Corp

When companies restructure, sustainability programs are often among the first things that quietly disappear. The 2025 zero-plastic goal was announced when the balance sheet looked different. The 2029 net-positive-nature commitment was made before the debt level reached its current point. The ethical sourcing contracts were signed before brands were put up for sale. You are here to ask: which of those commitments survives today's strategy, and which ones just became negotiating chips?

What You Care About
  • Whether cost cuts gut sustainability programs. Circularity initiatives, sustainable materials sourcing, and emissions reduction programs all require capital. When capital is being redirected to debt service and restructuring, something has to give. What is it?
  • Brand divestiture and ESG continuity. When a brand is sold to a private equity firm or a strategic acquirer, there is no automatic transfer of sustainability commitments. A buyer optimizing for short-term returns may abandon ethical sourcing or exit circularity programs entirely.
  • The gap between stated goals and operational reality. Public commitments and operational performance are not always the same thing. The COO has information about what the company is actually doing that does not appear in any press release.
  • Greenwashing risk. A company making ambitious public sustainability targets while simultaneously slashing the budgets required to meet them has a legal and reputational exposure. That story ends badly.
  • Workforce as a social dimension of ESG. The "S" in ESG includes how a company treats its workers. A workforce reduction plan that hits frontline workers harder than the corporate center is an ESG issue, not just a financial one.
Where to Look
  • Brand portfolio document: Each brand's stated sustainability goals and timelines are described. Note which brands have the most specific commitments and which ones are on the divestiture list.
  • COO interview (Alex Rodriguez): He knows the operational reality — water usage in manufacturing, transport emissions, packaging recyclability rates, and where current performance stands relative to public commitments.
  • CMO interview (Gail Peterson): She manages the public-facing sustainability narrative. Ask what commitments have been made and what resources are budgeted to meet them.
  • Income statement: Restructuring charges and SG&A trends show whether operational budgets are expanding or contracting. Sustainability programs live inside those budgets.
Your Outside Edge
  • Look up the Fashion Transparency Index or Higg Index scores for comparable apparel companies. Where do sustainability leaders in this industry actually set the bar? How do AI Corp's stated goals compare to what best-practice companies are actually achieving?
  • Research what happens to corporate ESG commitments in apparel sector restructurings historically. When Gap or J.Crew went through restructuring, what happened to their sustainability programs? What do PE-acquired brands typically do with inherited ESG commitments?
Your Deliverable

At the end of class, write one paragraph: ESG Risk Assessment. Does the proposed strategy maintain, weaken, or actively undermine AI Corp's sustainability commitments? Identify the single highest-risk ESG exposure in what you heard today — whether it is a specific commitment at risk, a greenwashing scenario, a divestiture without ESG protections, or a workforce impact that raises social responsibility concerns.

J♦ Stakeholder

Blackthorn M&A Partners

"Every brand in this portfolio is worth something. The question is whether management knows what."

Who You Are

You are an investment bank specializing in mergers, acquisitions, and divestitures in the consumer goods and apparel sector. You advise on both sides of deals: helping sellers maximize proceeds and helping acquirers avoid overpaying. You have seen what happens when a company buys at the top of a market and sells at the bottom. Your job is to make sure that every buy/sell/invest recommendation in today's pitches is grounded in something a real buyer would actually pay.

Your Stake in AI Corp

The portfolio decisions being recommended today are not strategic abstractions. They are transactions — and transactions have prices, buyers, timelines, and market conditions. When a consulting firm says "divest Brand X for $Y billion," your firm is the one that has to go find that buyer and defend that number. If the valuation is fantasy, you will not get the deal done.

What You Care About
  • Acquisition post-mortems. The company has a long history of buying brands. At least one purchase looks catastrophic. What do the write-downs tell you about whether management can value a business accurately?
  • Divestiture candidates. Several brands are already flagged for sale. Who is the realistic buyer universe — private equity, strategic acquirer, or no buyer at all? What multiple would they pay?
  • Goodwill and intangibles. Nearly a third of total assets are goodwill and intangibles. How much of that is still at risk of impairment? What does it mean for book value and for deal negotiations?
  • Proceeds allocation. If brands are sold, where does the cash go? Debt reduction, reinvestment, or return to shareholders? The answer changes whether a deal actually improves the company's position.

Where to Look

  • Balance sheet: Goodwill and intangibles balances across years reveal the pace of value erosion. Look at the trajectory since the largest recent acquisition was completed.
  • CFO interview (Martin Hughes): Ask about which brands have taken write-downs and why. Ask about flagged divestitures and what process is underway. Notice whether he can articulate the original acquisition rationale for the most troubled asset.
  • Financial ratios: The current enterprise value relative to EBITDA tells you how the market values the whole company. Individual brand transactions should be evaluated against that backdrop.
Your Outside Edge
  • Look up recent M&A transactions in the apparel and footwear space. What revenue or EBITDA multiples are comparable brands actually selling for right now? Streetwear, outdoor performance, and workwear each trade at different multiples.
  • Research who the likely acquirers are. Which PE firms are active in apparel buyouts? Which strategic buyers have announced intentions to expand in these categories? A brand is only worth what someone will actually pay today — not what it might be worth in three years.
Your Deliverable

At the end of class, write one paragraph: Valuation Opinion. Do you agree with the portfolio moves proposed? Name the one transaction that was best supported and the one that was most poorly priced or structured. What is the pitching firm getting wrong about the buyer universe or the achievable valuation?

K♠ K♥ K♣ Pitching Team Guide

Pitching Team Guide

How to Structure Your Presentation to CEO Bob Buckwalter

Your Pitch Structure
1
Open (30 seconds)

State your firm's thesis in one sentence. What is the core problem and what is your core solution? The room should know your position before you get into the details.

2
The $300M Bridge (1-2 minutes)

Break down where the savings come from. Name the levers. Quantify what you can.

"Cut costs" is not a bridge. "Consolidate 4 distribution centers to 2 for $45M, reduce corporate center overhead by $120M, vendor rationalization for $35M" is a bridge.

The numbers need to add up. The stakeholders in the room will check.

3
Portfolio Calls (1-2 minutes)

Which brands get investment? Which get sold? Which get harvested? Be decisive. The room will punish hedging. If you skip the hardest decisions, the M&A firm and the hedge fund will call you on it.

4
Implementation Sequence (1 minute)

What happens first, second, third? Why that order? What has to be true before the next phase begins? Sequencing matters because some moves unlock cash that funds the next move.

5
Close (30 seconds)

One sentence on what AI Corp looks like in 3 years under your plan.

What Will Get You Challenged
  • Vague language: "improve efficiency," "optimize operations," "leverage synergies"
  • Avoiding the hardest portfolio decisions
  • Numbers that do not add up to $300M
  • Ignoring what the executives told you, or not catching where they disagreed with each other
  • Forgetting that real people work at these brands. The labor coalition is in the room.
  • Assuming operational improvements without explaining how they happen. The supply chain team is listening.
Tips
