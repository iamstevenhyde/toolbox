Martin Gale — AI-Powered Deconstructed Case

Activity Brief and Grading Framework

What This Activity Is

Students act as newly hired consultants for Apparel Innovation Brands (AI Corp), a fictional company with a 12-brand portfolio of apparel and accessories. A newly appointed CEO, Bob Buckwalter, has announced a target of $300 million in cost reductions. He wants a real strategy, not a list of platitudes.

The "deconstructed case" format means students have no pre-written narrative to read. Instead, they assemble the picture themselves by interviewing three AI executive bots (CFO, CMO, COO), studying the financial statements, and reading the brand portfolio document. The bots are built on separate knowledge bases and are designed to subtly disagree with each other. Students who only interview one bot and read the income statement produce generic memos. Students who triangulate across all three executives and use financial evidence to test executive claims produce work that looks like actual consulting.

The Spring 2026 version runs as a Competing Consulting Firms live class session. Students prepare their analysis before class, then present in assigned firm roles while other teams play stakeholder roles (activist investor, credit rating analyst, labor coalition, press, M&A advisors, ESG watchdog, industry analysts).

Learning Objectives

  • Apply capital allocation logic to a distressed, multi-brand portfolio
  • Use financial statements diagnostically, not decoratively
  • Identify executive misalignment as a governance signal, not just a communication problem
  • Make decisive, defensible calls under uncertainty rather than hedging every recommendation
  • Adapt strategy when new information arrives mid-session

The Scenario

Apparel Innovation Brands (AI Corp) is at a critical juncture. CEO Bob Buckwalter has taken over a company carrying roughly 4.5x debt-to-EBITDA, a corporate center that grew 34% while revenue stagnated, and a 12-brand portfolio that includes two brands posing the defining capital allocation decisions of the turnaround.

Buckwalter wants consultants to tell him:

  1. How to revitalize the brand portfolio
  2. What operational changes will actually move the needle on efficiency
  3. How to accelerate digital transformation without destabilizing operations
  4. What to do about the broken U.S. wholesale channel
  5. How to hit sustainability targets while remaining solvent

The target: identify and bridge at least $300 million in credible cost reductions.

Assignment Instructions

Before Class

  1. Interview all three executives. Ask each one about the company's biggest problems, the $300M target, and the most difficult portfolio decisions. Pay attention when their answers conflict with each other or with the financial data.
  2. Analyze the financials. Use the balance sheet, income statement, and financial ratios to identify the key problems and quantify the opportunity. Do not stop at the $300M number — show where each dollar comes from.
  3. Read the brand portfolio document. Map each of the 12 brands: how big is it, how fast is it growing, what role does it play in the portfolio, and what would you do with it?
  4. Write your report. Approximately 500 words. Identify the problems, make your recommendations, and submit along with your bot chat transcripts by the deadline.

In Class (Competing Consulting Firms Format)

Three teams pitch their strategies to CEO Buckwalter (played by the instructor). Seven teams play stakeholder roles. Every team has a deliverable — nobody is just watching.

RoleCardWhat You Do

GreenMark SustainabilityQ♦ESG watchdog: hold firms accountable to sustainability commitments
Blackthorn M&A PartnersJ♦M&A advisor: challenge asset valuations
Larkwell & AssociatesJ♥Industry analyst: buy, hold, or sell on AI Corp stock
Slate Business JournalJ♣Press: one headline and two-sentence take on each pitch

After each pitch, the CEO asks one hard question, then the designated stakeholder teams respond. Role cards are available as a separate handout.

Mid-session, one piece of real-world outcome data is revealed. Teams in later rounds must reconcile their strategy with it. This is intentional. Adapting well to new information is part of what is being evaluated.

At the end, every team writes their position on two key brands (SELL or KEEP, with a price estimate) on the whiteboard. No hedging. No blanks.

The Two Decisions That Define the Case

Most students write generic cost-cutting memos. What separates strong work from weak work is making decisive capital allocation calls on two specific brands:

BrandThe Decision
DunnInvest approximately $100M to turn around manufacturing, or divest for approximately $1.8B? This is the biggest portfolio call. It drives the deleveraging path and determines what the company looks like in three years.
RankA $2.1B acquisition that has taken $735M in impairments. Keep and try to recover value, or sell after the write-down? This is a sunk-cost logic test.

Students who avoid these two calls produce work that cannot be acted on, regardless of how polished the prose is.

Grading Framework

Pre-Class Written Report (submitted before class)

DimensionWhat Strong Work Looks Like
Problem diagnosisUses specific financial evidence (ratios, dollar amounts, trend lines) to name the actual problems, not categories like "inefficiency" or "declining sales"
$300M bridgeBreaks the target into specific levers (corporate center reduction, SG&A rationalization, DC consolidation, working capital velocity, etc.) that sum to the target — not a restatement of the goal
Portfolio callsMakes explicit keep/sell/invest decisions on the major brands, especially Dunn and Rank, with defensible rationale — not "it depends" hedging
Executive evidenceUses executive interview transcripts diagnostically: quotes support or challenge financial analysis, rather than appearing as decoration or filler
Executive misalignmentIdentifies where the three executives disagree with each other, and uses that disagreement to draw a conclusion about governance or decision-making constraints
Implementation logicSequences recommendations with a rationale for why that order makes sense (e.g., cash generation before heavy restructuring spend)

In-Class Participation

RoleWhat Is Being Evaluated
Pitching firms (Kings)Decisiveness, financial grounding, ability to defend under pressure, coherent strategy logic
Stakeholder teamsStaying in role, asking questions that come from their specific lens (not generic "what about efficiency?"), building on what other teams said
Press (Slate)Synthesis: headline captures the actual tension or failure in the pitch, not a restatement of the firm's own claims
All teamsWhiteboard declaration: a committed position on both key brands

What Weak Work Looks Like

  • Generic language ("improve efficiency," "invest in digital") without quantification
  • Covering 1-2 of the 12 brands and ignoring the rest
  • Repeating the $300M target without showing where it comes from
  • Avoiding the Dunn and Rank decisions
  • Pasting executive quotes as decoration rather than as evidence
  • Treating the assignment as an operations memo rather than a capital allocation problem

Deliverables

Before class: Written report (~500 words) plus bot chat transcripts, submitted to Canvas by the deadline.

End of class (pitching firms): One paragraph stating what the other two firms got right that you missed, or got wrong that you caught.
